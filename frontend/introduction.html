INTRODUCTION

 

Your kidney is made up of nephrons. Nephrons are the filtering unit in kidney, they further include filters called glomerulus and tubule. Function of glomerulus is to filter your blood and tubule removes waste after returning back the needed substances.

INTRODUCTION TO CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE

 

Chronic kidney disease(CKD) occurs when your kidney is tired of doing its job. Chronic kidney disease is a condition where your kidney loose its function over time and also loose its progression. CKD can increase health risk and may lead to other diseases like heart disease, Hypertension, Bone disorders, Malnutrition.

CAUSES OF CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE

 

Chronic Kidney Disease(CKD) can be developed due to various factors. CKD develops gradually and may not noticeable in early stages. There are many causes which can develop CKD :

 

  • Diabetes: High blood sugar level can leads to the damaging of kidney blood vessels and its functions. It may cause blood vessels to become narrow and reduces blood flow to kidney.

 

  • Hypertension(High Blood Pressure): Hypertension leads to kidney blood vessel damage, glomerular damage, reduces renal blood flow hence leads to the acceleration of other kidney diseases such as diabetic nephropathy, glomerulonephritis.

 

  • Glomerulonephritis: Glomerulonephritis refers to inflammation of the glomeruli, the filtering units of the kidneys. Various factors, including infections, autoimmune diseases, and genetic predisposition, can cause glomerulonephritis and contribute to CKD.

 

  • Recurrent Kidney Infections: Chronic or recurrent urinary tract infections, especially if left untreated, can lead to kidney damage and scarring, increasing the risk of CKD.

 

  • Age and Genetic Factors: Age-related changes in kidney structure and function, as well as genetic factors, can influence the risk of developing CKD. Some individuals may be genetically predisposed to kidney disease or may inherit specific conditions that increase their risk.

 

NOTE: CKD often develops gradually over time and may not cause noticeable symptoms in the early stages. Regular monitoring of kidney function through blood and urine tests is essential for early detection and intervention.

 

SYMPTOMS CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE

 

  • Fatigue and Weakness: As kidney function declines, the body may struggle to produce enough red blood cells, leading to anaemia. Anaemia can cause fatigue, weakness, and a general lack of energy.

 

  • Changes in Urination: Individuals with CKD may notice changes in their urination patterns, such as:
  1. Increased frequency, especially at night (nocturia).
  2. Decreased urine output.
  3. Dark-coloured or foamy urine (due to proteinuria).
  4. Presence of blood in the urine (haematuria).

 

  • High Blood Pressure: CKD can cause or worsen hypertension, which can lead to complications if not managed effectively.

 

  • Nausea and Vomiting: The build-up of waste products in the blood (uraemia) can cause gastrointestinal symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, and loss of appetite.

 

  • Weight Loss: Individuals with CKD may experience unintentional weight loss due to loss of appetite, nausea, and vomiting

 

NOTE: It's important to note that these symptoms can vary in severity depending on the stage of CKD. Early-stage CKD may not cause noticeable symptoms, while advanced CKD can cause more pronounced and varied symptoms.

 

PREVENTIONS FROM CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE

 

  • Maintain a Healthy Diet: Follow a balanced diet low in salt (sodium), processed foods, and red meat. Increase your intake of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein. A healthy diet can help manage blood pressure, cholesterol, and weight, reducing the risk of CKD.

 

  • Control Blood Pressure: High blood pressure is a significant risk factor for CKD. Regularly monitor your blood pressure and take prescribed medication if necessary. Aim for a target blood pressure as recommended by your healthcare provider.

 

  • Manage Diabetes: If you have diabetes, work with your healthcare provider to keep your blood sugar levels under control. Proper management of diabetes can help prevent diabetic nephropathy, a major cause of CKD.

 

  • Maintain a Healthy Weight: Being overweight or obese increases the risk of diabetes, hypertension, and other conditions that can lead to CKD. Maintain a healthy weight through a balanced diet and regular physical activity.

 

  • Stay Hydrated: Drinking enough water helps your kidneys function properly by flushing out waste products. However, avoid excessive fluid intake, which can strain your kidneys .

 

  • Get Regular Exercise: Regular physical activity can help you maintain a healthy weight, lower blood pressure, and improve overall health. Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise per week.

 

  • Treat Urinary Tract Infections Promptly: If you experience symptoms of a urinary tract infection (UTI), seek medical attention promptly to avoid kidney complications.

 

  • Limit Alcohol Consumption: Excessive alcohol consumption can lead to high blood pressure and kidney damage. If you choose to drink, do so in moderation.

 

  • Monitor Kidney Function: If you have risk factors for CKD (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, a family history of kidney disease), ask your healthcare provider to monitor your kidney function through blood and urine tests. Early detection allows for timely intervention.

 

NOTE: By adopting these preventive measures, you can reduce your risk of developing CKD and maintain overall kidney health.
